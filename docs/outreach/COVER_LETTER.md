# Polaris Outreach Letter

**Naming:** Polaris is the public-facing research program; **NOESIS** is the
repository and implementation that hosts and supports that work. They are not
separate projects.

**Subject: Invitation to review an evidence-conservative methodology for AI
incident reconstruction**

Dear Research Team,

I am writing to introduce Polaris, an open research project investigating how
publicly documented AI incidents can be reconstructed from verifiable evidence
without turning incomplete records into claims of hidden reasoning, semantic
truth, or causality.

Polaris has completed its second milestone with the bounded scope recorded in
[ADR-038](../architecture/ADR-038-milestone-2-closure.md). Within a local
experimental trust model, the project has demonstrated deterministic
construction and cold replay of content-bound analytical trajectories,
fail-closed evidence validation, explicit preservation of unknowns and typed
lineage, and a built-in closed declarative evaluation boundary. These results
do not establish production readiness, human benefit, arbitrary-evaluator
independence, or the truth of an incident narrative.

The next research step is Pilot-001: a bounded, reproducible reconstruction of
one public AI incident. Before treating that pilot as methodological evidence,
we would value independent scrutiny of the source-selection rules, epistemic
labels, reconstruction procedure, threats to validity, and reproducibility
criteria.

We therefore invite a technical dialogue around a limited
[Independent Methodology Pilot](PILOT_PROPOSAL.md). The request is not for
funding, product adoption, or joint development. A useful outcome would be a
critical review, an independent rerun, or a documented falsification of one or
more methodological claims.

The concise project overview is available in the
[one-pager](POLARIS_ONE_PAGER.md), with further detail in the
[research summary](RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md) and [technical FAQ](FAQ.md).
